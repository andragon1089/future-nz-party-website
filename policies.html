Keeping Kiwi Doctors Home

What's the Issue?

New Zealand has an ongoing issue with the healthcare system. In a recent poll, New Zealanders ranked our healthcare system an average of 34/100. With a 38% deficit in residential nurses, healthcare systems are being overwhelmed. New Zealand is undeniably in a healthcare crisis, and changes must occur. Even now, hospitals in Dargaville, Kaitaia, and Kawakawa have over one in three positions empty and are forced to send critical patients to larger hosiptals by ambulance.

New Zealand's government has tried to solve this issue, but their efforts have been largely ineffective. Our government has recently invested $34.2 million into several nurse training facilities. However, 71% of those nurses go overseas within three years. What the government is addressing is capacity, not retention, leaving our healthcare system in a critical state. Health Minister Simeon Brown has acknowledged this crisis, yet efforts remain ineffective.

What Has the Government Tried?

In 2023, the National Government supplied medical training facilities with funding to increase the number of qualified nurses in New Zealand. However, this isn't effective - majority of the nurses that graduate with international licences leave New Zealand within three years and keep the healthcare crisis unresolved. For similar reasons, New Zealand currently has 0.8 fewer General Practitioners per 1000 people than Australia.

How we'll solve it

Our proposed solution tackles these problems with a five-year student debt cancellation policy. By offering students a way to erase what could be the largest debt of their life, we give New Zealand doctors an incentive to stay in New Zealand and support our healthcare system. For every year over 5 years, 20% of recently graduated medical students' debt will be cancelled. This can be reduced to 3 years by working in rural or understaffed regions. When these new doctors spend their first five years in New Zealand, they're far more likely to settle down and feed tax dollars back into the economy. Their presence will cut the need for expensive locum doctors, further reducing the financial burden of this policy.

Scheduling GP visits will be easier and more flexible; Emergency Department wait times will drop significantly, and every single Kiwi will have easier access to healthcare services. Our government's $220 million per year spending on locum doctors will drop, reducing costs, and rural regions will no longer have to ambulance patients to larger hospitals. With time, our healthcare system will fully recover, letting you and your family no longer have to worry about health.

Our proposed policy tackles massive issues that haven't been addressed by our current government. From keeping New Zealand-trained doctors home to providing needed support for rural hospitals and communities, the costs associated with our policy are completely outweighed by the benefits. New Zealand has several major crises, and Future NZ will work to solve them all, starting now.

Supporting New Apprentices

What's the Issue?

New Zealand is suffering from a lack of skilled workers. Our country needs more workers to handle our growing population. However, apprenticeship levels are consistently decreasing. In just three years, the number of workplace-based learners fell by 12%, leaving Kiwi homes, roads, and public buildings deteriorating. Without the backbone of our country, New Zealand will fall into ruin.

This issue is worsened by our emigrants. 80,040 Kiwis leave New Zealand yearly, and the majority of those people are able to work. We believe this problem is caused by a stagnant economy and a shrinking job market. Without action, this issue will spiral out of control and negatively affect generations of Kiwis to come. In one year, the number of apprentices dropped 14%, highlighting the decline of our workforce. To save our country, family, and friends, this issue cannot remain unresolved.

What Has the Government Tried?

Proposed by the Labour Party, the apprenticeship boost provides a subsidy of $500 per week to eligible companies per apprentice. This was to support the financial burden of both paying and training apprentices, and to support New Zealand-run companies. However, from 2024 to 2026, 8000 less people were supported by this boost. This shows its ineffectiveness. We believe this is because of higher expenses and rising living costs.

How we'll solve it

We propose a solid solution - raise the Apprenticeship Boost to $1000 per week. To counteract the rising costs of training apprentices, more money is necessary. Not only will this stimulate the job market, but it will also bring more highly skilled New Zealanders into the economy. Construction, which makes up 49% of all Kiwi apprentices, will experience a large influx of workers, lowering house prices and helping Kiwis own their own home. A $2500 tax credit will be given to those who begin working full-time for two years in areas of high demand. Although this will begin as an expensive project, the increased number of workers will return this money in tax, and Kiwi families can experience far lower costs of living without additional taxes.

We will also continue to incentivise Kiwis to achieve higher qualifications, as our Apprenticeship Boost will support companies and incentivise them to take on apprentices. This means that learning in the workplace will no longer be expensive for companies and will therefore result in more highly skilled workers. By voting for us, Future NZ, you can help fill the absence of new, skilled workers.

New Zealanders will begin to feel the lessened strain on construction and maintenance quickly - new, experienced workers will potentially reduce taxes and increase public infrastructure size and quality. Jobs will be created, the next generation of Kiwis will enter an active job market, and our economy will begin recovering.
